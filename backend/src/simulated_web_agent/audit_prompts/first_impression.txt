## Your task

Reason briefly IN ENGLISH as this person. For EACH dimension, first state what
*this* person specifically would notice, then score it. A 28-year-old marketer
who lives in online shops and a 61-year-old pensioner buying once a year do not
see the same page — if your scores would be identical for both, you have not
done the task.

## Scoring dimensions (score each 1–5)

- purpose_clarity: Would THIS person immediately understand what this site is
  FOR and what they can do here (see "What this site is" above — that may be
  selling, browsing a catalog, finding a store, or something else entirely)?
  1=completely unclear, 5=immediately obvious
- navigation_visibility: Are the main navigation options visible to THIS person?
  1=navigation hidden or absent, 5=prominent and intuitive
- call_to_action: Is the primary action obvious and compelling to THIS person?
  1=no CTA visible, 5=CTA prominent and compelling
- visual_hierarchy: Does the layout guide THIS person's eye logically?
  1=chaotic or flat layout, 5=clear hierarchy leading the eye
- trustworthiness: Does the design inspire confidence in THIS person?
  1=untrustworthy / amateurish, 5=highly professional and trustworthy

Scoring discipline — read carefully, this is where evaluators go wrong:

- You are not reviewing the site to be fair to its makers. You are recording how
  well it serves THIS person. Politeness is a scoring error.
- Calibrate against the scale, not against "is this a normal website":
    5 = nothing about this dimension could be improved for this person
    4 = works for them, with a minor friction
    3 = they manage, but it costs them effort or a second look
    2 = a real barrier; they hesitate, misread, or nearly give up
    1 = fails them outright
  A competent, unremarkable page is a 3. Most real pages score 2–4. A 5 is rare
  and must be earned — if you are giving several 5s, you are being lenient.
- Before scoring each dimension, name the single biggest barrier this person
  faces on it. Every dimension has one. If you cannot find it, look harder at
  the screenshot — do not award a 5 to avoid the work.
- Your scores must not contradict your own reasoning. If this person distrusts
  online payment, or cannot read small text, that MUST show up in the relevant
  score — not only in the prose.
- Score only what is visible in the screenshot. If you cannot point at the
  evidence, you are guessing.

overall_score = average of the 5 dimension scores, rounded to 1 decimal.
